Worm Food Circuits

Only MC, the pharyngeal pacemaker, reliably encodes feeding (— of — animals), and it sits farther from food-odour and longevity neurons in the synaptic wiring than chance would predict.

Computational companion to the AINN thesis on food-odour perception and dietary-restriction (DR) longevity in C. elegans. Public whole-brain recordings and connectomes.

The question

Which neurons could carry news of food to the circuits that set a worm's lifespan?

Eating less extends lifespan in many animals, including the worm C. elegans. Tanisha Handoo's AINN thesis asks how merely perceiving food, through smell, changes that benefit. This project asks the same question at whole-brain scale, without a lab, using recordings and wiring maps that are already public.

What we used

Whole-brain calcium recordings from freely moving worms (Atanas et al. 2023; Dag et al. 2023), which capture most head neurons at once alongside behaviour. Synaptic wiring maps and predicted neuropeptide networks for every neuron.

What we did

Tested which neurons track feeding, beyond speed and head bending, and which respond when a worm reaches food. Then measured how close those neurons are in the wiring to odour-sensing and longevity neurons, compared with random neurons of similar connectivity.

What it produces

A ranked list of candidate neurons and peptide routes, with experiments the lab can run. It generates hypotheses. It doesn't test lifespan directly, because no lifespan was measured here.

What the findings tell us

A specific feeding signal

About —% of head neurons track pumping overall, but only MC, the pharynx's own pacemaker, does so consistently (— of — animals). MC transmission needs eat-2, and eat-2 mutants are the standard genetic model of dietary restriction, so it already has a link to DR biology.

Food entry is tangled with behaviour

Up to —% of neurons pass the test at food entry, and fewer once behaviour is removed. But response sizes shrink only modestly, and entry was identified from the worm's own slowing, so these data can't separate food from behaviour.

A bottleneck in the wiring

MC is farther than chance from odour and longevity neurons, on average — and — synaptic steps. RIP is the only synaptic bridge out of the pharynx. A short synaptic route from feeding to lifespan circuits looks unlikely.

Peptides are the lead

Neuropeptides don't need synapses. One model predicts — MC links to longevity neurons against — by chance. The other models and datasets only partly agree, so this is a lead to test, not a result.

What this means for the thesis

Smell enters through AWA and AWC, while eating is reported inside the pharynx by MC. These may be separate routes to lifespan circuits, which is a hypothesis the thesis experiments with diacetyl can test.

The next experiment: does blocking peptide signalling from MC change the lifespan benefit of DR, with and without food odour?

What it can't tell us

  • Whether feeding signals actually reach longevity neurons.
  • Whether any of these neurons causes a change in lifespan.
  • Which neurons respond at food encounter, because those animals have no neuron identities.
  • Anything about weak encoders in the — animals without enough pumping variation.

Where the neurons in this project sit

The pharynx has its own small nervous system. Sensory neurons sit behind the nerve ring and send long dendrites to the nose.

MC paces pumping and is the only class this project finds encoding it consistently. M3 and I5 tune pump timing, and NSM senses food being swallowed.

AWA and AWC detect food odours such as diacetyl at a distance, before the worm reaches food.

Sensory neurons linked to lifespan. Losing ASI or ASG extends life, ASJ and ASK promote longevity, ablating AWA and AWC prolongs life, and ASI is required for DR longevity. AFD acts at warm temperature.

One shortest synaptic route from MC to each neuron. In the Cook connectome RIP is the only connection between the pharynx and the rest of the nervous system: without it, MC can't reach any of these neurons. Most shortest routes then pass through ADF. This bottleneck is why MC is farther from these neurons than chance.

Proposed experiments

Readouts from the AINN thesis: lifespan under DR, pumping rate and DAF-16::GFP nuclear localisation, each with and without diacetyl. Revised after the peptide disagreement was found, and accepted by both reviewers.

Strongest evidence

MC: does the pumping pacemaker signal to longevity neurons through peptides?

MC transmission needs eat-2, and eat-2 mutants are the standard genetic DR model. MC is synaptically far from the longevity list, so a direct signal is more likely peptidergic. Both datasets predict links to ASG, ASJ and AWA; only the older one reaches ASI.

Lifespan of wild type, flp-21 and npr-5 mutants, fed and under DR, with and without diacetyl, plus an MC-specific flp-21 rescue. Measure pumping in every strain, since a pumping defect is itself a form of DR.

The GFP control found a false-positive problem

GFP neurons can't encode behaviour, yet one GFP animal passed the test because pumping declined while its fluorescence faded. Adding time-trend terms fixed it, at the cost of lower agreement with the authors' own pumping labels.

Limits to keep in mind

  • — of — animals lack enough pumping variation to test feeding encoding.
  • Food-entry times come from behaviour, so behaviour and food contact can't be separated, and the encounter animals have no identities.
  • Connectome distance can't show whether a link is used; peptide links are predicted from expression.
  • Matching on connection counts doesn't control where a neuron sits in the body.
  • Unique R² can exceed 1, so class means are sensitive to outliers. The frozen ranking uses means; medians are shown as a check.
  • AWA and AWC are in both the odour and longevity lists, so those two tests overlap in the main analysis.
